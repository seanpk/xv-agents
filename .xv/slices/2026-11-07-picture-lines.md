# One sentence per line in the picture

## Scenario

1. Each sentence in `.xv/picture.md` starts on its own line. The extra lines of a list item are indented two spaces, so they stay in the item.
2. When the lines are joined again, the picture's text is the same as before, word for word. A check shows this.
3. The briefs that write the picture (guardian, maker, watch, `xv-init`) state the rule: one sentence per line, and list-item lines indented two spaces. A repo that adopts xv gets the rule too.
4. `xv-check` finds the same things in the new picture as it did before.
5. A `CHANGELOG.md` line names the rule.
6. When a step leaves a picture line that holds more than one sentence, the packet gets a note naming the line. It is a note, not a stop. A sentence end counts only outside backticks, and only as a full stop and a space followed by a capital letter.

### Amended 2026-11-07 by Sean Kennedy

At the maker's stop, Sean chose how a list item counts. These points change how points 1, 3 and 6 are checked. They loosen no may-not line and no binding decision.

7. A list item's own marker at the start of a line is not a sentence end: `-` or `*`, or a number and a full stop such as `1.` or `12.`, after any indent. This holds for point 1's check (in `tests/test_picture_lines.py`) and for point 6's packet note. A full stop and a space followed by a capital letter later in the same line still counts as a sentence end.
8. A list item's extra lines line up with the item's text: two spaces after `- `, three after `1. `, four after `10. `. This replaces "indented two spaces" in point 1 and in the brief rule of point 3, so the briefs say "line up with the item's text". It does not apply to nested lists, which keep their own indent.
